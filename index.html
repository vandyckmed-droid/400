    Ranking

    What each leg measures

    The universe is fixed: every name is ranked against all ~650 of them. Both settings change how that one cross-section is read, not which names are in it.

    Data

    Refreshed each weekday morning from Financial Modeling Prep, after the previous day's close. If it goes more than four days without a refresh the list says so.

    How the score works

      Whether the score has been worth following, and other readings of the universe, are kept outside the app in the research section: a point-in-time backtest of the top decile and a set of prototype indicators, refreshed on demand rather than daily.

      Universe

      The S&P MidCap 400, plus the 250 smallest S&P 500 members by market cap — large-cap by index membership, mid-cap by size. Those 250 are badged in the list. The line between the two indices is an index committee's decision rather than a size boundary, so crossing it is the point: a name is measured against everything of roughly its size instead of against whichever index happens to hold it.

      Membership is point-in-time: the history chart re-runs the full cross-section at each month or week end using the universe as it stood that day, with the S&P 500 tail picked on that day's market caps. A name that joined recently is scored as an outsider on earlier dates — where it would have ranked — and those bars are dimmed.

      Data: Financial Modeling Prep. A percentile is a rank against peers, not a return forecast. Not investment advice.
